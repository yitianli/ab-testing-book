# Part II: Inference and Evidence Problems {.unnumbered}

The second part focuses on evidence problems that can appear even when treatment assignment is randomized correctly.

Chapter 4 explains why repeated checking creates the peeking problem and how sequential methods address it. Chapter 5 extends the same idea to multiple testing, where many metrics, variants, or segments can turn random noise into apparently significant results. Chapter 6 discusses variance reduction methods such as stratification, regression adjustment, and CUPED, which help when sample size is limited. Chapter 7 turns to limited observation time: short-term experiment results may not capture long-term value, so teams need delayed metrics, time-path analysis, and sometimes long-term holdouts.

The goal of this part is to make experiment evidence more reliable. Randomization creates a fair comparison, but analysis choices, sample size, and experiment duration still determine whether the evidence is trustworthy.
