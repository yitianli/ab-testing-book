# Part VI: When Randomization Is Hard {.unnumbered}

The final part discusses how to reason about causal evidence when a clean randomized experiment is not available.

Chapter 16 introduces quasi-experimental methods such as difference-in-differences and synthetic control, emphasizing both their usefulness and the assumptions that make them credible.

The goal of this part is to show how experimentation thinking still helps when randomization is constrained. The evidence is usually less clean, so the assumptions and diagnostics become even more important.
